Ludwig van Beethoven

1770–1827 · Bonn 1770 – Vienna 1827 · Classical era

The bridge between the Classical and the Romantic era: he kept composing masterpieces even after he had become completely deaf.

Life

He was born in Bonn, Germany, in December 1770. His father, a court singer, pushed him to perform as a child prodigy on the model of Mozart.

In 1792 he moved to Vienna, where he studied with Haydn and became famous as a pianist with overwhelming improvisations. Around the age of thirty he began to lose his hearing: in 1802 he poured out his despair in a letter to his brothers, the “Heiligenstadt Testament”, but decided to keep writing.

He composed 32 piano sonatas, 9 symphonies, concertos and quartets. The Ninth Symphony, first performed in 1824 when he was already deaf, ends with the Ode to Joy, today the anthem of the European Union.

Portrait: Joseph Karl Stieler (1820), public domain, from Wikimedia Commons.

Curiosities

  • Für Elise was not published by him: the manuscript was found and printed only in 1867, forty years after his death. Who “Elise” was is not known for sure.
  • The name “Moonlight” is not his: it was given by the critic Ludwig Rellstab, who compared the first movement to moonlight on Lake Lucerne.
  • When he was almost deaf, he used ear trumpets and pressed a wooden rod against the piano to feel its sound.

At the piano

  1. Beethoven lives on contrasts: sudden soft and loud, accents, rests. Follow the written dynamics.
  2. In the first movement of the Moonlight Sonata change the pedal at every change of harmony, so the sound does not blur.
  3. In fast passages practise slowly with the metronome and raise the speed a little at a time.
