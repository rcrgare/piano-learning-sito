The great piano composers

The story of the composers of our pieces, from Bach to Rachmaninoff: who they were, curiosities about their lives and tips for playing their music.

Baroque

JBJohann Sebastian Bach1685–1750 · 2 piecesThe great master of the Baroque: organist, teacher and father of twenty children, he wrote the music every pianist still learns from today.

Classical era

WMWolfgang Amadeus Mozart1756–1791 · 2 piecesThe most famous child prodigy in history: in only 35 years he wrote more than 600 works, from symphonies to operas.LBLudwig van Beethoven1770–1827 · 5 piecesThe bridge between the Classical and the Romantic era: he kept composing masterpieces even after he had become completely deaf.

Romantic era

JBJohann Friedrich Burgmüller1806–1874 · 1 pieceThe composer of the “easy and progressive” studies: his short pieces are among the first real pieces you learn on the piano.FCFrédéric Chopin1810–1849 · 8 piecesThe poet of the piano: he wrote almost only for this instrument and invented a new way of making it sing.RSRobert Schumann1810–1856 · 3 piecesThe Romantic who dreamed of becoming a great pianist: a hand injury turned him into one of the great composers.GVGiuseppe Verdi1813–1901 · 1 pieceItaly's best-loved opera composer: his melodies have become popular songs all over the world.CHCharles-Louis Hanon1819–1900 · 1 pieceThe author of the most used technical exercises in the world: for 150 years pianists have warmed up their fingers with “the Hanon”.JSJohann Strauss II1825–1899 · 1 pieceThe “Waltz King”: with his orchestra he made Vienna and the whole world dance.EGEdvard Grieg1843–1907 · 1 pieceThe musical voice of Norway: he brought his country's folk songs and dances into the concert halls of all Europe.

Late Romantic and 20th century

CDClaude Debussy1862–1918 · 3 piecesThe painter of sounds: with new, shaded harmonies he opened the way to 20th-century music.ESErik Satie1866–1925 · 3 piecesThe eccentric of Montmartre: with a few slow, simple chords he wrote some of the most hypnotic melodies ever.SJScott Joplin1868–1917 · 2 piecesThe “King of Ragtime”: he brought together African-American rhythm and the form of classical music.SRSergei Rachmaninoff1873–1943 · 1 pieceThe last great Russian Romantic: a legendary pianist, with huge hands and unforgettable melodies.
