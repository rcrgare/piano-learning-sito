About the piece

The fifteen Two-Part Inventions (BWV 772-786) began as study material for Bach's son Wilhelm Friedemann: the first versions are in his notebook of 1720. In 1723 Bach wrote out the final copy, presenting them as a way to learn to play clearly in two voices and to develop good musical ideas.

The Invention No. 8 in F major opens like a canon: the theme leaps up through the F major chord and runs down in sixteenth notes, and the left hand imitates it an octave lower, one bar later. It is one of the most played inventions.

How to practise it

  1. Hands separately, seriously. Each hand has a real theme to play: learn both well on their own before joining them.
  2. A consistent touch. A common choice is slightly detached eighth notes and legato sixteenths: pick one touch and keep it the same in both hands.
  3. Fixed fingering: choose your fingers once and never change them, it is the secret of clean sixteenths.
  4. Speed up only when both voices stay clear and even, without one covering the other.

Frequently asked questions

Which invention should I start with?

Many teachers begin with No. 1 in C major, No. 8 or No. 4 in D minor: they are among the most accessible.

Do you use the pedal?

Usually not, or very little: in Bach the clarity of the two voices comes from the fingers.
