About the piece

Beethoven wrote the Sonata Op. 27 No. 2 in 1801 and published it the following year as Sonata quasi una fantasia, dedicated to Countess Giulietta Guicciardi. The name “Moonlight” came only after his death, from a comparison by the critic Ludwig Rellstab.

The second movement is an Allegretto in D-flat major with a central Trio: a gentle minuet that gives a breath of air between the two movements in C-sharp minor. Franz Liszt described it as “a flower between two abysses”.

How to practise it

  1. Legato and staccato. In the first bars the right hand alternates two slurred notes and a detached one: that is the character of the whole piece, so work on it from the start at a slow tempo.
  2. The Trio on its own. The middle section has accents shifted onto the weak beats: practise it separately, then link it to the return of the Allegretto.
  3. Hands together only when each hand is secure alone, starting at 60% as in the video.
  4. Use little pedal: this movement should sound light and clear, not blurred.

Frequently asked questions

Is it easier than the first movement?

It is shorter and has fewer pedalled notes, but it asks for more precision in the staccato touch. Many people find it a good next step after the Adagio.

How long is it?

About two to three minutes with the repeats, so it is a realistic goal after a couple of years of lessons.
