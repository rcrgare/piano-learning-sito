About the piece

Mozart wrote the Sonata in A major K. 331 around 1783 and published it in Vienna in 1784 with Artaria, together with the Sonatas K. 330 and K. 332. It is an unusual sonata: none of its movements is in sonata form.

The first movement is a theme with six variations. The theme, an Andante grazioso in 6/8, is a simple, singing melody in two halves, each one repeated. The last movement of the same sonata is the world-famous Rondo «alla turca».

How to practise it

  1. The 6/8 rhythm. Count two beats in each bar, each divided into three: the long-short figure of the melody should sway, not hurry.
  2. Thirds and sixths. The double notes in the right hand must be smooth and even: practise them alone, softly, until both voices really sound together.
  3. The left hand accompanies: keep it lighter than the melody, so the tune stays in front.
  4. Hands together only when each hand works on its own, starting at 60% speed as in the video.

Frequently asked questions

Is it the same sonata as the Turkish March?

Yes: the Turkish March is the third movement (Rondo alla turca) of the Sonata K. 331. The Theme you find here is the opening of the first movement.

Does the video include the variations?

No, only the Theme with its repeats. The six variations are harder: the Theme is the natural place to start.
