About the piece

The tune comes from Shchedryk, a Ukrainian folk song of good wishes for the new year: a swallow flies into the house and promises the master a rich year. The Ukrainian composer Mykola Leontovych made it into a choral piece, first performed in Kyiv in 1916.

In 1922 the Ukrainian National Chorus sang it at Carnegie Hall in New York; in 1936 Peter J. Wilhousky gave it English Christmas words, “Carol of the Bells”. Here we play Leontovych's melody, with the four-note motif that keeps ringing like a bell.

The arrangement was written by us for Piano Learning; the app also has a ★★ version with chords.

How to practise it

  1. Read the notes above and find the hand position: most of the time the fingers stay on five neighbouring keys.
  2. The B♭-A-B♭-G motif returns through most of the piece: learn those four notes well and you already have half of it.
  3. Add the left hand only when the right hand is secure, first at 60% as in the video.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to read music?

No: the notes are written in letters and the video shows which key to press and for how long.

Can I play it with one hand?

Yes, the right-hand melody alone is already recognisable. In the app, if you practise one hand, the app plays the other.
