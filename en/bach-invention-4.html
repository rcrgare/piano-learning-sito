About the piece

The fifteen Two-Part Inventions (BWV 772-786) began as study material for Bach's son Wilhelm Friedemann; in 1723 Bach wrote out the final copy, presenting them as a way to learn to play clearly in two voices and to develop good musical ideas.

The Invention No. 4 is in 3/8: the theme is a run of sixteenth notes going up and down, and the left hand picks it up one bar later. During the piece one hand holds long notes while the other keeps running.

How to practise it

  1. Even sixteenths. Use a slow metronome: every note of the scale must have the same weight, without rushing on the way down.
  2. Hands separately until each hand plays the theme without hesitation: the left hand has exactly the same work as the right.
  3. Long notes count: hold them for their full value while the other hand moves.
  4. Fixed fingering and a relaxed wrist: speed comes by itself when the movement is free.

Frequently asked questions

Is it harder than No. 1 or No. 8?

It is at a similar, intermediate level. It is short, but the fast sixteenths in both hands need even fingers.

How long is it?

Less than a minute: it is one of the shortest inventions.
