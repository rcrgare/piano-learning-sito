About the piece

The Sonata in C minor Op. 13 was published in 1799 as Grande sonate pathétique, dedicated to Prince Karl Lichnowsky, one of Beethoven's first patrons in Vienna. It is one of his best-loved early sonatas.

The third movement is a rondo (Allegro): the main theme returns between different episodes, one in E-flat major and a central one in A-flat major built on imitation between the voices.

How to practise it

  1. The theme first. It is the part that comes back most often: learning it well means you already have a good share of the piece.
  2. One episode at a time. They have fast scales and triplets in the right hand: practise them hands separately and very slowly.
  3. The left hand often has octaves and broken chords: keep the wrist relaxed so you do not get tired.
  4. Speed up only when a passage comes out clean three times in a row.

Frequently asked questions

Is it suitable for beginners?

No: it is an upper-intermediate piece. If you have already played the Pathétique Adagio or classical sonatinas, you can tackle it one section at a time.

Does the theme resemble the Adagio?

Many listeners notice a family likeness between the melodies of the sonata's movements: it is worth listening to the whole sonata before you start.
