About the piece

The Minuet is in the second Notebook for Anna Magdalena Bach, begun in 1725: a collection of family pieces copied by Bach's wife and others. For a long time it was attributed to Bach; today scholars consider it the work of the Dresden organist Christian Petzold.

In the notebook it is followed by the Minuet in G minor BWV Anh. 115, with which it forms a pair: the two are often played one after the other.

How to practise it

  1. Hands separately. The right hand has the melody, the left a second voice of longer notes: both are simple, but each must be secure on its own.
  2. Count in three. Say “one-two-three” out loud: the minuet is a dance, and the first beat gets a slight accent.
  3. Hands together slowly, first at 60% as in the video, bar by bar.
  4. Play the right hand with a light, slightly detached touch where the notes move by step: it will sound lively.

Frequently asked questions

So it is not by Bach?

Probably not: in 1979 the musicologist Hans-Joachim Schulze identified it in a harpsichord suite by Christian Petzold. It remains forever linked to the Bach family notebook, though.

Is it suitable for beginners?

Yes, it is one of the best classical pieces after the first months of lessons: few notes at a time and a comfortable hand position.
