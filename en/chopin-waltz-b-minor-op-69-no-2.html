About the piece

Chopin composed it in 1829, while still in Warsaw, and never published it. It appeared only after his death, in the edition prepared in 1855 by his friend Julian Fontana: that is why the opus number 69 does not match the date.

The main section in B minor is nostalgic; in the middle comes a more relaxed episode in B major, before the theme returns.

How to practise it

  1. The waltz left hand. Bass on the first beat, chord on the second and third: practise the jump on its own until the hand finds the keys without looking.
  2. The right-hand melody has small ornaments: learn them slowly and evenly first, then let them flow.
  3. Hands together at 60%, keeping the left hand softer than the melody.
  4. Add rubato (slowing down and speeding up a little) only at the end: the left hand stays as steady as possible.

Frequently asked questions

Is it Chopin's easiest waltz?

It is among the most approachable, together with the posthumous Waltz in A minor. After a few years of lessons you can play it without big technical problems.

Why is it called “posthumous”?

Because it was published after Chopin's death; he had kept it in a drawer. The opus number was given by the publisher.
