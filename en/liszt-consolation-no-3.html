About the piece

Liszt's six Consolations were published in their final version in 1850. The title comes from a collection of poems by Charles-Augustin Sainte-Beuve, published in 1830.

No. 3, in D-flat major and marked “Lento placido”, is the most famous: no virtuoso display, but a broad melody and an arpeggio accompaniment that asks for close control of tone and pedal.

How to practise it

  1. The accompaniment first: the left hand alone, soft and even, until the arpeggios flow without bumps.
  2. Make the melody sing: its notes must be heard above all the others, even when the hand that plays them also accompanies.
  3. Pedal at every change of harmony: listen and change it when the sound starts to blur.
  4. Practise phrase by phrase and slowly: the difficulty is not speed, it is calm.

Frequently asked questions

Is it very difficult?

It is simpler than most Liszt, but not for beginners: it needs independence between melody and accompaniment and good pedalling.

How long is it?

About four minutes in our version.
