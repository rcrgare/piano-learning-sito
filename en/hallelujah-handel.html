About the piece

It is the chorus that closes Part Two of Messiah (HWV 56), the oratorio Handel composed in London in little more than three weeks, between August and September 1741, on Bible texts chosen by Charles Jennens. It was first performed in Dublin on 13 April 1742.

The words come from the Book of Revelation. Tradition says audiences stand for the chorus because King George II stood up in London in 1743, but there is no record of it from the time. Our arrangement follows the soprano part: the “Hallelujah” in D and in A, then “For the Lord God omnipotent reigneth” in A and in D.

The arrangement was written by us for Piano Learning; the app also has a ★★ version with chords.

How to practise it

  1. Read the notes above and find the hand position: most of the time the fingers stay on five neighbouring keys.
  2. In the “Hal-le-lu-jah” motif the first note lasts one and a half beats: count “one, two-and, three-and” and play the second note on the “and” of two.
  3. Add the left hand only when the right hand is secure, first at 60% as in the video.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to read music?

No: the notes are written in letters and the video shows which key to press and for how long.

Can I play it with one hand?

Yes, the right-hand melody alone is already recognisable. In the app, if you practise one hand, the app plays the other.
