About the piece

Mozart wrote the Fantasia in D minor in Vienna around 1782 and never finished it: the manuscript breaks off shortly before the end. It was published only after his death, in 1804; the closing bars played today were added in the first editions, probably by August Eberhard Müller.

The piece has the freedom of an improvisation: it opens with slow arpeggios (Andante), continues with an intense Adagio broken by rapid cadenzas (Presto) and ends with a serene Allegretto in D major.

How to practise it

  1. The opening arpeggios should be calm and pedalled, as if breathing: let them ring.
  2. The fast cadenzas (Presto) are free: practise them slowly, in groups of notes, then let them run without stiffness.
  3. The Adagio lives on rests and contrasts: respect the silences, they count as much as the notes.
  4. The final Allegretto changes character: light touch and clean staccato.

Frequently asked questions

Is it difficult?

It is upper intermediate, but much loved by those who have already played a few sonatinas: the fast passages are short and can be practised in pieces.

Why is it called a Fantasia?

Because it follows no fixed form: the sections follow one another freely, like an improvisation at the piano.
