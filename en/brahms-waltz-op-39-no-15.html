About the piece

Brahms wrote the 16 Waltzes Op. 39 in Vienna in 1865 and published them the following year with a dedication to the critic Eduard Hanslick. They were first written for piano four hands; Brahms himself then prepared the versions for solo piano.

No. 15, in A-flat major, is by far the best known: the melody moves in thirds and sixths above a bass that marks the first beat of every bar.

How to practise it

  1. Count in three and lean only on the first beat: the other two should stay light, like a Viennese waltz.
  2. Right hand alone until the thirds and sixths sound together, with no note arriving before the other.
  3. The bass softer than the melody: the left hand accompanies, it does not cover.
  4. Start slowly with a metronome and speed up only when the phrases stay connected.

Frequently asked questions

Is it suitable for a beginner?

It is an intermediate piece: short and without fast passages, but it asks you to play two notes together in the right hand with the same weight.

Is there an easier version?

Yes: Brahms himself also published a simplified version of the waltzes, where No. 15 is in A major. Here we play the original in A-flat.
