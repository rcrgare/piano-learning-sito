About the piece

Beethoven worked on the Symphony in C minor Op. 67 between 1804 and 1808; it was first performed in Vienna, at the Theater an der Wien, on 22 December 1808, in the same very long concert that also premiered the Sixth Symphony.

The whole first movement (Allegro con brio) grows out of the opening four-note motif, which passes from instrument to instrument and never lets the listener go. Here we play it in a reduction for solo piano.

How to practise it

  1. The opening motif: three short, even notes, then a long held one. Count carefully: the rest before the three notes is part of the rhythm.
  2. Practise in sections: exposition, development, recapitulation and coda. Each has its own passages to isolate.
  3. Full chords should be played with the weight of the arm, not the strength of the fingers.
  4. Keep the tempo steady: in this movement the energy comes from regularity, not from rushing.

Frequently asked questions

Can a symphony be played on the piano?

Yes: piano reductions were made precisely so that people who could not hear an orchestra could get to know the symphonies. Some colours are lost, but all the musical ideas remain.

Is it suitable for beginners?

The opening motif is; the whole movement is upper intermediate because of its chords and fast passages.
