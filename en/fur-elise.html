About the piece

Für Elise is a bagatelle in A minor that Beethoven wrote in 1810 and never published: it was printed only in 1867, forty years after his death. Nobody knows for sure who “Elise” was; a much-discussed theory is that the name is a misreading of “Therese”, his pupil Therese Malfatti.

It is a small rondo: the famous theme comes back three times, alternating with two different episodes, a calm one in a major key and a more restless one with a repeated note in the bass.

How to practise it

  1. The theme alone, right hand. Keep the E–D♯ oscillation light and try not to look at the keys. It is the part everybody knows, so learning it well pays off immediately.
  2. Add the left hand. The left-hand arpeggios fit between the right-hand notes: the hands almost never play together, which makes it easier.
  3. The middle episodes come later: they need more independence between the hands and a quick scale up the keyboard.
  4. Playing at 50–60% without mistakes is worth more than playing fast with errors. Then speed up a little at a time.

Frequently asked questions

Is Für Elise hard for beginners?

The main theme is within reach after a few months of lessons. The middle episodes are intermediate: many people learn the theme first, and that is a great goal.

Can I learn it without reading music?

Yes: with falling notes you see which key to press and when. Keeping the score next to you will help you learn to read music over time.
