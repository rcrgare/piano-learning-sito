Robert Schumann

1810–1856 · Zwickau 1810 – Endenich 1856 · Romantic era

The Romantic who dreamed of becoming a great pianist: a hand injury turned him into one of the great composers.

Life

He was born in Zwickau, Germany, the son of a bookseller. He studied law to please his family, but gave it up for the piano and took lessons from Friedrich Wieck in Leipzig.

Around 1832 an injury to a finger of his right hand ended his dream of a career as a pianist: he turned to composition and music criticism, founding in 1834 a music journal in which he would introduce young talents such as Brahms.

In 1840 he married Clara Wieck, his teacher's daughter and a great pianist, after a court case against her father. In his last years he suffered from serious mental illness and died in an asylum in Endenich, near Bonn.

Portrait: Josef Kriehuber (1839), public domain, from Wikimedia Commons.

Curiosities

  • Scenes from Childhood (1838) is not music for children but an adult's memories of childhood: the most famous piece is “Träumerei” (Dreaming).
  • The Album for the Young (1848) began as a birthday present for his daughter Marie: it is full of short pieces for young pianists, such as “The Happy Farmer” and “The Wild Rider”.
  • He signed his articles with two invented characters: the fiery Florestan and the dreamy Eusebius.

At the piano

  1. Every piece has a precise character, given by its title: before playing, imagine the scene.
  2. In “The Happy Farmer” the melody is in the left hand: it must sound louder than the right-hand chords.
  3. Look for a warm, singing tone even in the simple pieces.
