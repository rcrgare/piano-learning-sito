Claude Debussy

1862–1918 · Saint-Germain-en-Laye 1862 – Paris 1918 · Late Romantic and 20th century

The painter of sounds: with new, shaded harmonies he opened the way to 20th-century music.

Life

He was born in Saint-Germain-en-Laye, near Paris. He entered the Paris Conservatoire at ten and in 1884 won the Prix de Rome, the most important prize for young French composers.

He rejected the traditional rules of harmony and looked for new colours, inspired by poetry, painting and even the Javanese music he heard at the Paris World's Fair of 1889.

He wrote the opera Pelléas et Mélisande, the symphonic poem La mer and a great deal of piano music, including the Preludes and the Études. He died in Paris in 1918, during the First World War.

Curiosities

  • He is called an “impressionist”, like the painters Monet and Renoir, but he did not like that label.
  • Clair de lune is part of the Suite bergamasque and takes its name from a poem by Paul Verlaine.
  • The two Arabesques are early works, written between 1888 and 1891: his flowing style can already be heard.

At the piano

  1. Look for a soft, shaded sound: the fingers sink into the keys without striking them.
  2. The pedal is part of the colour, but change it when the harmony changes.
  3. The tempo is flexible: let the music breathe without losing the thread.
