About the piece

The three Nocturnes Op. 9 were published in 1832, shortly after Chopin arrived in Paris, and are dedicated to Marie Pleyel, wife of the piano maker Camille Pleyel. The second, in E-flat, is the most famous; the first is the most intimate.

It is marked Larghetto. The middle section moves to D-flat major with a simpler theme, almost a folk song, before the opening melody returns.

How to practise it

  1. The left hand alone. The arpeggios cover a wide part of the keyboard: practise them slowly until the hand moves without jerks.
  2. The right-hand runs (groups of 11, 20 and more notes over one accompaniment) are not counted strictly: spread them naturally over the left-hand chords.
  3. Hands together very slowly, section by section, at 60% as in the video.
  4. Pedal: change it with the left-hand harmony, usually once or twice per bar.

Frequently asked questions

Is it harder than Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2?

Yes, it is generally considered a little more demanding because of the runs and the wider left hand. If you have learned No. 2, you are ready to try it.

How do I play the small notes?

They are written-out ornaments: fit them freely over the accompaniment, starting and ending together with the left-hand notes shown in the score.
