About the piece

Beethoven wrote it as a young man, around 1795-1798, but it came out only after his death, in 1828, published by Diabelli. The manuscript was not quite finished and the edition was completed by another hand.

The joking title “Die Wut über den verlorenen Groschen”, the rage over a lost penny, appears on the manuscript but is not in Beethoven's writing. It fits the character, though: a simple theme that travels through every key, more and more agitated.

How to practise it

  1. Play the repeated notes of the theme by changing fingers (3-2-1), not with the same finger: they stay light even when fast.
  2. The theme comes back many times in different keys: learn it well the first time and recognise it each time it returns.
  3. Practise the fast sections slowly with a metronome, then speed up a little at a time.

Frequently asked questions

Is the title Beethoven's?

No. The nickname is on the manuscript, but written by someone else. Beethoven's title is “Rondo a capriccio”.

How hard is it?

It is an advanced and long piece. In the app you can practise it one section at a time and slow it down until the notes are secure.
