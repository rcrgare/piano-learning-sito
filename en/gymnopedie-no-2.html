About the piece

Erik Satie wrote the three Gymnopédies in 1888. The second is marked Lent et triste, “slow and sad”, and it is the only one of the three that Claude Debussy did not orchestrate.

As in the others, the left hand alternates a low note and a chord in 3/4 time, while the melody moves slowly above with a few long notes.

How to practise it

  1. The left hand alone. Low note on the first beat, chord on the second: practise the jump slowly, watching where the hand lands.
  2. The melody has long notes: count them carefully, Satie's music lives on pauses and waiting.
  3. Hands together slowly, with the melody a little louder than the chords.
  4. With the pedal, join the low note to the chord and change it at the start of every bar.

Frequently asked questions

Is it harder than No. 1?

The level is similar: slow tempo and few notes. If you have played No. 1, you can learn it quickly.

Can I play it on a digital keyboard?

Yes. With a sustain pedal connected it sounds closer to the original, but it works well without one too.
