About the piece

It is the last of the 12 Études Op. 10, published in 1833 and dedicated to his friend Franz Liszt. Tradition links it to September 1831, when Chopin, far from home, heard that Warsaw had fallen to the Russians after the Polish uprising.

The nickname “Revolutionary” is not Chopin's own. Like all his études it is both an exercise and a concert piece: here the work is all for the left hand, with long, very fast scales and arpeggios.

How to practise it

  1. Left hand alone first, very slowly, in groups of four notes: find a comfortable fingering and then never change it.
  2. Pass the thumb under smoothly: that is where fast scales usually stumble.
  3. The right hand has chords and octaves: keep the arm relaxed and let the weight fall, without pushing.
  4. Put the hands together slowly with a metronome; the real tempo comes last.

Frequently asked questions

How hard is it?

It is an advanced piece: it needs an already agile left hand. With the app, though, you can practise it one passage at a time, slowed down, until the notes are secure.

Why is it called an étude?

Études are pieces written to train one specific difficulty. Chopin also made them concert music: this one trains speed and independence in the left hand.
