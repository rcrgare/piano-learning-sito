About the piece

Joplin published Solace in 1909 with the subtitle A Mexican Serenade. Instead of the usual ragtime bass, the left hand plays a habanera rhythm, the dance of Cuban origin.

It became famous again in 1973, when Joplin's music was used in the film The Sting and his work was rediscovered by the wider public.

How to practise it

  1. The left-hand rhythm. The habanera has one long note, one short and two even ones: tap it on the table before you play it.
  2. The right-hand melody is singing, with a few syncopations: learn exactly where the accents fall.
  3. Hands together at 60%, one section at a time: the piece has several themes that come back.
  4. Do not rush: Joplin asked for his pieces never to be played too fast, and that is truer here than ever.

Frequently asked questions

Is it a rag?

Yes and no: it is written in Joplin's style, but the rhythm is a habanera and the tempo is slow. For many it is his most expressive piece.

Is it easier than The Entertainer?

The level is similar; the slow tempo helps, but the left hand needs a precise rhythm. If you have played The Entertainer, you can tackle it.
